1. PROJECT OVERVIEW
    ============================================================

Project Barnabas is a Christian youth technology camp designed to combine:

* Christian faith and discipleship
* Technology education
* Computer science
* Cybersecurity awareness
* Electronics and making
* Robotics
* Outdoor recreation
* Team building
* Creativity
* Service
* Responsible and ethical use of technology

The camp is intended to operate as a companion week to the church’s existing summer camp.

The existing church camp would take place during its normal week spanning late July/early August. Project Barnabas would ideally take place during the following week so that the existing camp kitchen and related infrastructure can remain available and then be cleaned up afterward.

The camp would use approximately 40 acres of family-owned property, subject to applicable property restrictions, homeowners association rules, zoning, permits, insurance requirements, and other legal requirements.

The primary technology and gathering facility would be an existing barn.

Approximately 1.5 acres of additional property would be designated as an outdoor recreation and activity area.

============================================================
2. VISION

To create a Christian environment where young people can discover that technology is not merely something they consume, but something they can understand, build, create, and use to serve God and other people.

Project Barnabas should encourage campers to become:

* Curious
* Creative
* Responsible
* Technically capable
* Spiritually grounded
* Ethical
* Collaborative
* Service-minded

============================================================
3. MISSION

Project Barnabas exists to provide youth with practical technology education in a safe Christian community while teaching them to use technology responsibly, creatively, and ethically.

The camp will combine structured instruction, independent exploration, worship, recreation, fellowship, and hands-on projects.

============================================================

FAITH

RESPONSIBILITY

CURIOSITY

CREATIVITY

Campers should be encouraged to build rather than merely consume.

SAFETY

5. CAMP PHILOSOPHY

The camp should intentionally balance three different experiences:

1. STRUCTURED
2. EXPLORATORY
3. SPIRITUAL/COMMUNAL

The morning should be highly structured.

The middle of the day should provide significant freedom for campers to explore stations and outdoor activities.

The later portion of the day should transition toward worship, reflection, fellowship, and community.

The goal is not to create another traditional classroom.

The goal is to create an environment where campers can learn by doing.

17. CYBERSECURITY PRINCIPLES

Campers should learn that cybersecurity is fundamentally about protecting people and systems.

Core principles:

* Get permission first.
* Know what you are authorized to test.
* Never attack someone else’s system.
* Never steal credentials.
* Never deploy malware.
* Never disrupt real services.
* Never invade someone’s privacy.
* Practice only in controlled environments.
* Report vulnerabilities responsibly.

All practical cybersecurity exercises should use intentionally vulnerable systems owned or controlled by the camp.

25. FIRST-YEAR PRINCIPLE

DO NOT TRY TO BUILD EVERYTHING AT ONCE.

The first camp should focus on creating an excellent experience rather than an enormous technology program.

A successful first year might consist of:

* A functional computer lab
* A small networking lab
* A basic electronics station
* Several outdoor games
* Strong worship
* Good meals
* Safe supervision
* A handful of excellent projects
* A final project showcase

If campers leave saying:

“I didn’t know I could do that.”

“I want to learn more.”

“I made something.”

“I met people who like the same things I do.”

“And I learned something about God.”

then Project Barnabas has succeeded.
