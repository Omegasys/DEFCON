Hacking Conference – Features, Experiences & Swag

1. Conference Identity and Philosophy

The goal is to establish a recognizable hacking and cybersecurity conference with its own identity, traditions, physical experiences, and collectible equipment.

Rather than simply copying existing conferences such as DEF CON, the intention is to take inspiration from the broader hacking conference community and develop original features that attendees associate specifically with this conference.

Core principles:

* Hands-on learning and experimentation.
* Privacy, security, and decentralization.
* Hardware and software exploration.
* Community participation.
* Collectible and practical conference merchandise.
* Memorable experiences that continue beyond the conference itself.

2. Interactive Conference Experiences

2.1 Wall of Sheep Alternative

Develop an original educational exhibit inspired by the concept of the Wall of Sheep.

Possible implementation:

* Display anonymized examples of network misconfigurations.
* Demonstrate insecure network practices.
* Teach attendees how information can leak.
* Explain how to prevent common security mistakes.
* Focus on education rather than publicly shaming individuals.

Possible name: Misconfiguration Map.

2.2 Challenge Rooms

Dedicated rooms where attendees can participate in controlled cybersecurity exercises.

Possible activities:

* Blue team defensive security challenges.
* Capture the Flag (CTF) competitions.
* Intentionally vulnerable machine environments.
* Network troubleshooting challenges.
* Hardware hacking exercises.
* Linux installation and configuration workshops.

2.3 Privacy Scavenger Hunt

A conference-wide activity involving privacy and security challenges.

Possible activities:

* Find tracking mechanisms.
* Identify insecure configurations.
* Solve cryptographic puzzles.
* Locate hidden clues throughout the venue.
* Complete challenges using conference equipment.

2.4 Packet Detective

An interactive networking exhibit where attendees learn to investigate network traffic.

Possible activities:

* Analyze sample packet captures.
* Identify suspicious connections.
* Investigate simulated network incidents.
* Learn to recognize common protocols.
* Solve networking mysteries collaboratively.

2.5 Live Build Clinics

Practical workshops where attendees build, configure, or repair technology.

Possible activities:

* Install Linux distributions.
* Configure privacy-focused operating systems.
* Build LoRa devices.
* Configure networking equipment.
* Experiment with microcontrollers.
* Learn basic electronics and soldering.

3. Physical Conference Features

Develop original physical experiences and equipment that attendees can interact with, collect, and use.

Possible features:

* Interactive electronic conference badges.
* Modular physical badges.
* Challenge rooms.
* Networking equipment demonstrations.
* Hardware hacking stations.
* Privacy and security demonstration stations.
* Conference-wide wireless mesh network.

5. Included Meshtastic / RNode Device

One of the central ideas is to include a physical LoRa networking device with every standard admission ticket.

The device would be separate from the electronic conference badge.

5.1 Purpose

Every attendee receives their own networking device, allowing them to experiment with decentralized communications during and after the conference.

Potential benefits:

* Introduces attendees to LoRa technology.
* Encourages decentralized networking.
* Creates opportunities for hands-on learning.
* Allows attendees to communicate without conventional cellular infrastructure.
* Builds a community of people who own and understand mesh networking equipment.
* Gives attendees a useful piece of hardware to take home.

5.2 Potential Platforms

Meshtastic:

* Off-grid messaging.
* LoRa mesh networking.
* Local communications.
* Conference announcements.
* Educational networking challenges.

RNode / Reticulum:

* Decentralized networking.
* Resilient communication infrastructure.
* Networking across different communication mediums.
* Experimentation with alternative network architectures.

Both ecosystems could eventually be supported.

5.3 Conference-Wide Mesh Network

The conference could establish its own temporary mesh network.

Potential features:

* Attendee-to-attendee messaging.
* Conference announcement channels.
* Strategically positioned relay nodes.
* Dedicated networking challenges.
* Mesh networking demonstrations.
* Educational workshops.
* Optional network visualization.

Privacy considerations:

* Avoid publicly identifying node owners.
* Use appropriate regional radio configurations.
* Provide unique private channel configurations where applicable.
* Document security limitations.
* Avoid distributing shared credentials as though they guarantee privacy.
* Follow applicable FCC regulations and radio-frequency requirements.

5.4 Annual Collectible Nodes

The physical nodes could have annual editions.

Each year could feature:

* A different enclosure design.
* A unique color scheme.
* A different conference edition marking.
* An annual identification number.
* Updated hardware or firmware where practical.

The intention is to encourage attendees to collect equipment from multiple conference years.

6. Attendee Entrance Package

The standard admission package would include:

1. Electronic Conference Badge
    * Primary conference identification.
    * Interactive features.
    * Potential electronic puzzles and challenges.
2. Personal Mesh Networking Node
    * Separate from the badge.
    * Meshtastic or compatible RNode hardware.
    * Intended for continued use after the conference.
3. Connectivity Kit
    * USB cables.
    * Adapters.
    * Cable ties.
    * Small connectivity accessories.
4. Equipment Pouch
    * Reusable carrying case.
    * Storage for the badge, node, cables, and accessories.
5. Additional Swag
    * Conference merchandise.
    * Collectible items.
    * Educational materials.

Develop a system where equipment and merchandise from each conference year have their own theme.

Possible annual themes:

Year 1 – Foundation

* Basic connectivity equipment.
* Conference identity.
* Initial electronic badge.

Year 2 – Transmission

* LoRa networking.
* Mesh networking.
* Wireless communications.

Year 3 – Hardware

* Electronics.
* Embedded systems.
* Hardware hacking.

Year 4 – Cryptography

* Hardware authentication.
* Cryptographic challenges.
* Security equipment.

Year 5 – Decentralization

* Distributed networking.
* Alternative communication systems.
* Decentralized infrastructure.

Equipment from different years could remain interoperable wherever practical.

8. Core Equipment Philosophy

The conference should distinguish between its different physical components.

* Badge: Who you are at the conference.
* Mesh Node: How you communicate.
* Connectivity Kit: How you connect your equipment.
* Tools: How you experiment and build.
* Equipment Pouch: How you carry everything.

The objective is to make the conference feel like an annual technology gathering where attendees receive an actual toolkit, rather than simply a bag of promotional merchandise.

Overall Vision:

Create a hacking conference that people remember not only for its talks and competitions, but also for its physical experiences, networking infrastructure, community, and useful technology.

Attendees should leave with equipment they can continue using, knowledge they can apply, and an experience that makes them want to return the following year.
